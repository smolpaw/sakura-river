# Performance

What the renderer does for speed, what was tried and rejected, and what is still open. Distilled from the 2026-09-27/28 performance work: the engine was moved from WebGLRenderer to WebGPURenderer + TSL (three 0.186.1) and then tuned for GPUs from integrated to discrete. Research notes with sources are in `docs/research/`.

## Targets

- Devices from the last 3–4 years (owner, 2026-09-28): discrete desktop GPUs, modern integrated GPUs (Intel Iris Xe/Arc, AMD 680M-class, Apple M-series) and current mobile GPUs, on WebGPU. The WebGL2 fallback need not match it in features or speed (owner, 2026-10-02). Older hardware is out of scope.
- On integrated GPUs, memory bandwidth decides performance. The main costs are render-target formats, MSAA, fullscreen passes, shadow-map size and overdraw.
- The owner's desktop (RTX 2060) is a personal machine, not a bench rig. Check changes with a few stills (see CLAUDE.md), and run long GPU timing only when the owner says it's a good time.

## In place

Each item passed the visual gate or is pixel-identical to what it replaced.

- **Blossom depth prepass:** an alpha-only depth pass, then the lit pass with depthWrite off, with a constant 4-unit depth offset. It was the largest single saving (scene pass 10.4 → 9.1 ms at 4K). A slope-scaled offset let near-coplanar cards through and failed the gate.
- **30 Hz updates:** the wind-animated shadow map and the water reflection render every other frame, on every tier.
- **Effect buffers per CSS pixel above DPR 1.4:** light shafts at 0.5/DPR, reflection at 0.35 at DPR 2 (0.25 fails near the water). No change at DPR 1.
- **Uniform branches** keep features that are off from costing anything: terrain underwater terms above the water line, lantern/temple/bridge lamp light before dusk, raindrop rings when dry, lightning. Rain streaks draw a count that scales with intensity, and none when dry.
- **Small models in thousands: merge, don't instance.** three's instancing keeps the matrices in a uniform buffer up to 64 KB (1,024 instances) and switches to per-instance attributes beyond (`nodes/accessors/Instance.js`). For 2,110 copies of a 24-triangle stone (the village's walls) that path cost ~1.4 ms a frame (WebGL, 1080p, RTX 2060; the first ~1,000 cost nothing); merged into a few meshes (`lods.js makeMerged`) they cost nothing measurable. The woods, bamboo and gorge walls stay under 1,024 per draw; the blossoms, turf and wildflowers are over it (the turf and wildflowers together ~0.15 ms). (On WebGPU the Blender models' instanced draws keep their matrices in a storage buffer instead: Start-up.)
- **Overall (2026-10-01, the grass, village, farmland, shrubs and waterwheel together):** GPU time against f7b3a13 (WebGL, 1080p, high, RTX 2060, interleaved A/B, 90 frames): hero 5.03 → 5.56 ms, cine000 ±0, cine250 4.71 → 5.30, cine500 4.56 → 5.07, cine750 ~5.0 → 5.35; triangles 6.4–6.6M → 8.0–8.5M, draw calls +1 to +27. Generation ~0.69 → ~1.05 s on 4 workers (desktop).
- **The valley's shadow map** (`farShadow` in `src/sunshadow.js`, 2026-10-01): a second sun shadow map over a 960×970 m box round the valley (8192 on ultra, 4096 on high, 2048 medium, 1024 low; 0.12–0.9 m texels), drawn from casters on layer 3 (the terrain and farmland, the woods, bamboo, gorge walls, village, temple, bridge, boulders and walls, the cherries' trunks and flower shadow proxies) with three's shadow-pass override material (back faces, each object's own positionNode and alpha cut-out). The sun moves only during a time-lapse (the clock doesn't tick), so it is redrawn only then, once the sun has moved more than ~0.04° and at most every 4 frames: a redraw costs ~1–1.7 ms GPU and ~2 ms CPU (WebGL, 1080p, RTX 2060, every frame against none). It is not drawn with the sun down, except on the warm-up: the page opens at night, and without that the first time-lapse past sunrise built every caster's pipeline for it in one frame (~20 node builds, a 0.1–0.5 s freeze). The lighting model multiplies the sun by it, and the hand-made sun terms (backlit grass, blossom and petal glow, water glints) follow; one shared node, so a material using it twice samples once. One bilinear compare per map, not four: on WebGL three gives every texture node with a flipY uniform a per-object update every frame (`TextureNode.update`), and four lookups in every lit material cost +26% CPU per frame (5.4 → 6.9 ms). Steady state against the commit before (three interleaved runs, hero/cine250/cine750): GPU 0.97× [0.80, 1.18], CPU 1.05× [0.78, 1.32].
- **Cloud shadows** (`cloudShadow` in `src/sunshadow.js`): a few interfering sine waves per lit pixel on a cloud layer 900 m up, folded into the same shared sun-shadow node; skipped (uniform branch) below cover 0.15 and above 0.95. Three value-noise octaves there cost +1.5 s of pipeline compilation at start-up (headless WebGPU, 7.9 → 9.4 s), one octave +0.8 s; the waves +0.45 s.
- **Rain wetness** (`LitMaterial.setupVariants` in `src/tsl.js`, puddles in `terrainMaterial`): behind uniform branches, nothing while the ground is dry; wet, a few multiplies per lit pixel, and on the ground two noise lookups (the puddle mask) and, inside puddles only, the paddies' water shading.
- **The valley's shadow map's lookup** goes through `MapLookup` (`src/sunshadow.js`, flips uv itself on WebGL), so three gives it no per-object update: −5% CPU per frame. Rejected (2026-10-02): cross-fading two maps so each redraw's new texel grid doesn't tick the shadows: +0.7 s of start-up (a second lookup in every material) and +0.25 ms GPU for a tick ~1% above the frame-to-frame noise of the grass and water.
- **Petals simulated in a compute pass on WebGPU** (2026-10-02, `src/petalsgpu.js`): the CPU system's flight (wind, gusts, flutter, landing on the grass, floating down the river) in storage buffers, 2.5× the tier's petals (9,000 on high) shed by every cherry instead of the main tree only, less the quarter the main tree holds back (`MAIN_SHED` in `src/main.js`); landing reads the terrain grid the grass stands on (`grass.userData.groundAt`), the river's path is the same analytic curve as `world.js`. WebGL keeps the CPU system. Against the CPU petals (WebGPU, 1080p, headed, interleaved): cine250 over five runs GPU 0.99× [0.88, 1.15], CPU 0.82× [0.69, 0.92]; a three-run pass over hero/cine250/cine750 had read GPU 1.13× with intervals reaching 0.83, which the longer run did not bear out. Start-up +0.2 s.
- **River bed and waterwheel white water** (2026-10-03): the bed's stones and gravel (`riverBed` in `src/materials.js`) are two 3×3 cellular lookups (18 hashes) on terrain pixels below y = 0.08 within 60 m only (a branch); the wheel's churn and trail (`makeWater`, `wheel`) one extra noise lookup per water pixel. With the blossoms' petal transmission and pink shade (`blossomLight`), against the clouds commit (WebGPU, 1080p, three runs): GPU 1.02× (CIs spanning 1), CPU ±0.
- **Texture nodes on WebGL:** `matrixAutoUpdate` off on data and depth textures sampled with explicit uv (the light map, the valley's shadow map): otherwise three recomputes their uv transform per object per frame.
- **Scene uniforms in the render group** (`U` in `src/tsl.js`): one shared buffer per render instead of one per object. No measurable change on the bench (CPU 0.94× [0.71, 1.19]).
- **Overall (2026-10-01, the realism pass: aerial haze, softer grade, the valley's and the clouds' shadows, reeds, night sky, rain wetness, leaf cards, hearth smoke and sparks, Fuji):** against 8a7f94c (WebGL, 1080p, high, RTX 2060, four interleaved runs): GPU 1.04–1.07× over hero/cine000/cine250/cine500/cine750 (geomean 1.05×, every CI spanning 1), CPU 1.04–1.14× (geomean 1.09×). Start-up (headless WebGPU, `createMs`, mostly pipeline compilation): 6.6 → 8.3 s; the steps: aerial haze +0.25, shared uniforms +0.1, the valley's shadow +0.3, cloud shadows +0.45, reeds +0.1, wetness +0.2, smoke and sparks +0.2 s.
- **Few render items:** WebGPURenderer pays per render item (three #30560: CPU per frame went ~1.3 → ~3 ms in the port; still GPU-bound). New content goes in instanced or merged draws: the birds in flight are six instanced draws (one per species), lanterns one, the temple one, the woods three per tree kind (24), the gorge's walls two per wall (6), the bamboo three per stand (6).
- **Grass placed on the GPU** (`src/grass.js`, 2026-10-01): no per-blade data. Blades stand on world-fixed cells around the camera in levels (each level's cells twice the size of the one before), each with a rank; a blade is kept while its rank is under (d0 / distance)² times the ground's density and shrinks into the ground as that falls past it, so every level costs about the same and the grass reaches 240 m on five levels (high). Blades beyond d0 widen and turn to the camera, and their roots and normals take the ground's tone, so thinning leaves no specks. Each level is one instanced draw over the tiles of its 8×8 grid in range and in view (CPU culled per frame, tile list in a uniform array); faded-out blades skip the vertex work. The quality controller's grass steps (0.7, 0.5) scale the density and cull each level's tiles at √f of its range, where its blades would have faded anyway, so fewer instances run at all. Blades sit on the terrain mesh itself (its grid heights in an RGBA32F texture, triangle interpolated after inverting the grid's sinh spacing), and the same per-vertex grass values colour the terrain, which also carries the blades' wind waves and backlight. GPU time (WebGL, 1080p, RTX 2060, interleaved A/B, against the old 32k CPU-placed clumps in a ~70 m oval): +0.02 to +0.18 ms per frame over hero/cine250/cine750. The first terrain shader for it cost +0.35 ms on its own (four extra noise lookups per pixel); sharing two octaves with the ground detail and limiting the blade streaks to 40 m removed most of that. 0.43–0.83M grass triangles submitted, about half collapsed (faded out).
- **Woods level of detail** (`src/lods.js`, also the gorge's walls, switched at 70 m): each tree kind has a full model for trees within 90 m of the camera, a `_far` one to 200 m and a `_dist` one beyond (5 m of hysteresis at each switch); the instances are re-sorted into the draws when the camera has moved 4 m (or turned 5°: frustum culling, below). The far models carry 20% of the crown's triangles (conifers a quarter, from fewer, bigger tufts so their tiers survive), the distant ones 6% (conifers 9%), fused on a voxel grid twice as coarse before decimating, since at that budget the fine one decimates into shards. Most trees are 300–800 m from the usual cameras, so the distant level carries most of the woods: it paid for 2.2× the trees (1,214 → 2,614 on high) at the same triangle count.
- **Frustum culling of the Blender models and impostors** (2026-10-04, `makeView` in `src/lods.js`): every level of every kind (the Ultra tier's near models and the woods' leaf cards included) and the impostor cards are culled per instance. The re-sort that rewrites each level's instance buffers when the camera has moved 4 m also runs when the view has turned more than 5° (or the field of view has changed as much), and puts each level's instances in view first (`vis` of them, in their lists' order) and the others after them (to `all`). Each level's `onBeforeRender` gives it `vis` for the camera and the river reflection's camera and `all` for every other camera, so the sun's sharp map and the valley's map still draw every caster: an instance out of view keeps its shadow, and the maps are unchanged (depth is the nearest whatever the order). Rejected: shadow-only copies of the levels on their own layer (twice the render items) and culling against the union of the view and the sun's frustum (the valley's map covers the whole valley, so the woods would not be culled at all). The test is a bounding sphere per instance (all its kind's levels and leaf cards together; for an impostor its card's corners) against the camera's four side planes widened by 10° and pushed out by 4 m, so what the camera sees before the next re-sort (under 4 m and 5° away) is always inside; a sphere also passes if its mirror image in the river's plane is inside, since the reflection's camera is the camera mirrored in that plane. The levels of detail are still chosen from where the camera was when it last moved 4 m, so a turn never switches a level and culled frames match unculled ones exactly. Levels only the shadow maps draw (the woods' far models behind the impostors) are not culled. The village's merged walls are culled whole per group by three (`frustumCulled`, per pass), the grass by its tiles, the cherries' flowers and the turf whole; the wildflowers, the fallen petals and the petal rafts are not culled (~75k, ~60k and ~71k triangles on high, near the tree and along the river). `setCulling(false)` on the engine handle turns it off; `info().cull` gives the re-sorts, their mean main-thread time and per group the instances the camera draws.
  - Checked pixel-identical, culled against unculled in the same frame (headless WebGL, 1280×720): 60 views on high and Ultra (hero, cine250, cine750, first-person views along the lanes facing four ways at 17:53 and 09:12, the sun behind included, views down onto the river, and the camera moved 3.8 m and turned 4.5° from where the lists were built, just short of a re-sort), and 6 views headed on WebGPU. The checks catch the faults they guard against: without the margins the moved views pop at the frame's edges (0.4–50k pixels), without the mirror test the river loses reflected trees (2–4.6k pixels), and with the shadow maps drawing only the lists in view the shadows of trees out of view vanish from the slopes (with the valley's map redrawn every frame). Culled frames also match the build before culling pixel for pixel (6 views).
  - Counts (high, WebGL): the camera draws 2,150–2,650 of ~4,650 instances (models, impostor cards, wall groups) in the hero and cinematic views and 700–2,540 walking, the models' triangles in its pass 400–510k of 930–980k and 240–540k of 0.9–1.2M walking. Triangles per frame over all passes (two frames, as the 30 Hz shadow and reflection passes alternate): hero 5.92 → 5.24M, cine250 5.65 → 4.87M, cine750 5.51 → 4.79M, walking −0.7 to −1.3M (−11 to −30%). Ultra: the models' triangles in the camera's pass 2.0–2.9M → 0.64–1.16M. GPU time not measured.
  - CPU: a re-sort of every group (~4,650 instances on high) takes 0.31 ms on average, 0.23 ms without culling (headless Chromium on the desktop, 1,200 re-sorts). Most of it is copying instances into the buffers; a slot that already holds the same instance is skipped, and a level none of whose slots changed is not uploaded, so a turn leaves the shadow-only levels alone. Turning re-sorts every 5°, so a fast drag can re-sort nearly every frame.
- **Adaptive quality** (`src/quality.js`): the render scale (floor 0.6), then reflection resolution, grass density and reflection off. The scene pass, and with it the shafts, bloom and the reflection's buffer, render at the scale and FSR 1 upscales the tone-mapped image to the drawing buffer before the grade (`Upscaler` in `src/post.js`, three's FSR1Node: EASU, then RCAS at sharpness 0.5; off at scale 1, when its passes are skipped). Before (2026-10-08) the canvas itself shrank and the browser stretched it bilinearly; at scale 0.6 against the native image (headless WebGL, 1280×720, high, mean ꟻLIP, lower is closer) FSR reads 0.067 against 0.089 (hero, night), 0.064–0.069 against 0.086–0.092 (cine250 night, hero and cine750 day 0.082 against 0.085, 0.074 against 0.076): edges (the pagoda, the lantern ropes, blades) hold where the stretch blurs them. It costs the upscale (0.34 ms at 1080p on the RTX 2060) and the grade at the drawing buffer's size instead of the scaled one (+0.22 ms): the hero view at scale 0.6 on WebGPU 4.99 ms against 4.40 stretched and 7.35 at scale 1, so the controller's model (70% of the frame scaling with pixels) still holds. Temporal upscalers were rejected at full resolution (below); FSR is spatial, so swaying foliage and alpha-to-coverage keep their edges. `create(canvas, { upscale: false })` keeps the stretch (the ultra tier, which supersamples, scales the canvas as before); `setRenderScale(s)` sets the scale for captures. Tiers below ultra at scale 1 are pixel-identical to before (hero and cine250, day and night). It is driven by GPU timer queries where they exist, otherwise by frame time with a vsync-safe target. GPU timings arrive 10–30 frames late, so samples of frames rendered before a change are ignored; acting on them made the loop oscillate. A median picked the upper mode of the alternating 30 Hz frames, so the estimate is a trimmed mean. It steps up only when its model predicts staying 5% under target, and ignores the first 1.5 s (pipeline compilation).
  - **30 fps rung** (2026-10-08, owner: weak devices should see more of the scene, and a constrained device gives up frame rate before detail): with the frame rate on Auto and GPU timings available, a frame over budget at 60 fps halves the frame rate first (the budget per frame doubles to 28 ms), at the highest scale that fits it, and only then does the controller go on to 0.6 and the second-order settings. At 30 fps the sun's shadow map and the reflection are drawn every frame, so they keep their 30 Hz. In the controller's simulation (`bench/sim-controller.mjs`): a device at 30 ms a frame at scale 1 settles at 30 fps and scale 0.85 instead of 60 fps and 0.6; 10 ms over at 1080p, 30 fps at scale 1 instead of 60 at 0.9; 30 ms over, 30 fps at 0.75 instead of 60 at the floor; a device just over budget (17 ms), 30 fps at scale 1 instead of 60 at 0.9. Without GPU timings (frame time only) there is no rung: a capped frame can't tell how far under budget it is. The visitor can hold 60 or 30 instead (`setFrameRate`, the page's frame-rate menu); 30 applies on hand-picked tiers too. (`halfAt` in `src/quality.js` below 1 would lower the scale that far at 60 fps first.)
  - **A GPU with time to spare lowers its clocks, so its frame times grow** (measured 2026-10-08, RTX 2060, hero view at 1080p): at 60 fps it runs at 1935 MHz (memory 6801, P2, 42% busy) and a frame reads 7.5–9.5 ms; held at 30 fps it drops to ~1080 MHz (memory 5000, P3, 38% busy) and the same frame reads 13–14.5 ms. The governor keeps the GPU about 40% busy, so a frame's GPU time at 30 fps hardly follows the scale until the load nears the budget, and a frame that would fit 60 fps can read as not fitting. So from 30 fps the controller returns to 60 at once only when a frame at scale 1 fits 60's budget with the dead band to spare, and otherwise, with clear headroom (under 80% of 30's budget), on trial: 60 fps at the same scale, measured after ~1 s (the clocks rising), back to 30 at once if over budget, the next trial twice as far off (24 s, 48 s, … up to 6.4 min at 30 fps). Forcing the rung on this desktop with a tiny budget (`targetMs` 6, under the governor's ~13 ms) made the scale wander at 30 fps, as the readings sat over that budget at any scale; the real budget (28 ms, 85% of the frame) is far above the governor's level, so readings exceed it only when the GPU is busy at full clocks. Not checked on a weak device (none here).
- **Starting tier from adapter/renderer strings** (`detectTier` in `src/main.js`): software and old mobile GPUs start on low; Intel integrated, AMD APUs, Apple, ARM/Qualcomm, WebGPU compatibility-mode devices and ≤4-core machines on medium; the rest on high. Never on ultra: that is only picked by hand.
- **Start-up:** at least 24 hidden warm-up frames behind the loading veil build every pipeline (`compileAsync` only knows the canvas target) and absorb a one-off stall three shows around frame 16. Every feature that is off at start is forced on during warm-up so its pipeline exists. Worst visible frame on WebGPU went 580 → 23 ms. Procedural generation runs in a pool of inline workers (critical path ~0.35–0.5 s), so WASM isn't worth it.
  - The scene's parts come in over the warm-up's frames (2026-10-08): the rest are hidden by their layers, a frame brings in at least a twelfth of them and more while frames stay short, and the frames that bring parts in draw every pass (shadow maps, reflection). Drawn all at once, the first frame was one main-thread task of three's node building for every object and pass: 12.4 s with the CPU throttled 4× (headless WebGL, low; ~3 s unthrottled), long enough for a browser to call the page unresponsive; spread out, the longest task is 2.1 s there (0.6 s unthrottled), total time unchanged. The frames are drawn at a quarter of the resolution, so a weak GPU spends little on them.
  - One shader build per material and vertex layout for the instanced models (2026-10-08, `shareInstancedPipelines` in `src/lods.js`, WebGPU only): three keys a render object's node build (the WGSL generation that the first frame spends its main-thread task on) by an InstancedMesh's uuid, since the build's bindings hold that mesh's matrix buffer (three PR 29066), so every level of every kind built its own, once per pass. With the matrices and colours in storage buffers the build is the same for all of them: the key leaves the uuid out and each render object's bindings are pointed at its own mesh's buffers (three recreates a bind group whose attribute changed). The colours are stored four floats wide: three pads a vec3 storage array to that on upload and swaps in its own array, which left the re-sort writing into a detached one (black models). Against the commit before (headless WebGPU, high, 1920×1080, three interleaved runs): the first frame's task 3.83 → 2.12 s, time to first frame 11.05 → 9.22 s, `createMs` 7.15 → 7.17 (headed 1280×720: 5.1–5.5 → 4.4–4.6 s). Pixel-identical, headed WebGPU: hero and cine250 by day and night, the walk view, cine750.
  - Shader preparation (`compileAsync`) reports its progress to the loading screen, and a minute without an object done ends the start-up with an error the page shows: a pipeline the driver rejects could otherwise leave it waiting for ever (it did on Android's compatibility mode, Cross-GPU correctness).
- **Shader size** (2026-10-08): TSL inlines every `Fn` without `setLayout` at each call and a JavaScript `for` loop unrolls into the shader, so the value noise (`vnoise`, ~60 calls of four hashes), the water's twelve touch points, the grass's ten farmland zones and the lanes' and river bed's 3×3 cell searches were copied many times over: the water's fragment shader was 188 KB of GLSL, the farmland's 150 KB, the terrain's 140 KB, the grass's vertex shader 75 KB. Slow drivers (ANGLE's D3D11 path on Windows, mobile GLES) compile big shaders slowly, long enough for a browser's GPU watchdog to reset the device. `hash12` and `vnoise` are now shader functions, the touches and the zones a `Loop` over uniform arrays, the cell searches functions with a loop: largest shader 188 → 46 KB, all of low's WebGL shaders 2.94 → 2.33 MB; headless WebGL `createMs` high 5.9 → 5.0 s, low 5.3 → 4.6 s; the site's load with the CPU throttled 4× (WebGL, low) 31 → 23 s. Captures pixel-identical (high and low, three views at three times, a close-up of the heron's wakes and a ring, two farmland views) except the cell searches: in a loop the compiler can't fold the cells' constant offsets into the sums, and 2.1k pixels of the walk view (at pebbles' edges) differ by 1–3 levels, a few dozen more; the same pebbles side by side. `bench/shaders.mjs` lists a tier's shaders by size.
- **One pipeline per material for the instanced models** (2026-10-04, `shareInstancedPipelines` in `src/lods.js`, WebGPU only): three keeps an InstancedMesh's matrices in a uniform array sized to its count and named after its node's id, so every level of every kind had its own vertex shader and pipeline and compiled its material's fragment shader again (the boulders' stone shader 11 times, the gorge's 6). The matrices now sit in a storage buffer (a runtime-sized array) under one name, so a material's instanced draws share one pipeline: 121 → 56 pipelines at `create()` (headless WebGPU, high), 539 → 246 counting the warm-up's. Medians of three interleaved runs against the build before (and the commit before the day's six features, 8fd8ba6): `createMs` headless WebGPU 10.9 → 7.6 s (8.7), headed WebGPU 1280×720 8.2 → 6.6 s (7.0), Ultra headless 13.4 → 8.9 s (one run); with the 24 warm-up frames included, headless 15.9 → 12.8 s (12.8), headed 16.1 → 11.4 s (13.7). WebGL keeps three's path (no storage buffers there; headless WebGL `createMs` 4.8 → 4.5 s from the generation changes below, 4.3 before the six features). Captures pixel-identical, WebGL and headed WebGPU (high and Ultra, day and night, near stone). Not timed on the GPU per frame (a storage read in place of a uniform read per vertex). What the warm-up's first frame still spends (~5 s headless) is three building each render object's node graph on the main thread, once per object and pass (an instanced mesh is keyed by its uuid); sharing pipelines does not touch that, and the six features add ~1 s of it.
- **Generation:** the lanes' distance grid (`laneDist` in `src/world.js`, made by every worker on its first height) is filled segment by segment within 8 m of each (the same values; ~50 ms less per worker); the village's dry-stone walls' joints (`stoneJoints`, ~0.4 s in Node for their ~9,800 stones) are worked out in the rocks' job, which waits for the walls' stone models' vertices (`modelVertices`, loaded meanwhile; a job's args may be a promise, `src/gen/pool.js`), not on the main thread. Buffers identical (bench/hashcheck.mjs, high and low).
- **What the 2026-10-04 features cost at start-up** (headless WebGPU `createMs`, each left out in turn, with the shared pipelines): the stone close up ~0.4 s (its three shaders, ~45 KB of WGSL each), the boat, fireflies, butterflies and herons together ~0.4 s, the wayside under 0.1 s; all six ~0.9 s.

- **Multisampled colour discarded after the resolve** (2026-10-08, `discardMultisampleStores` in `src/post.js`, WebGPU): three stores the scene pass's 4× colour attachment after resolving it, which nothing reads; on tile GPUs (Apple, Adreno, Mali) the store writes four samples a pixel to memory where the resolve alone keeps them on the tile. The RTX 2060 shows no difference (hero and cine250 pixel-identical, headed WebGPU); the saving on a tiler is unmeasured (no such device here). The depth attachment is still stored.
- **Bounded clocks** (2026-10-14, `runClocks` in `src/main.js`, the loops in `src/tsl.js`): the scene's running time, the wind's run and the river pattern's run are kept in doubles on the CPU and handed to the shaders wrapped, `uTime` every 2000π s (~1.75 h), `uWindRun` every 50, `uFlow` every 2000 (~26 min at the default river), the clouds' shadows' drift every 62.5π. Every shader use repeats exactly over its loop, so a wrap shows nothing: rates nudged by under 1/6283 to whole turns (`loopW`, `loopRate`, `loopPeriod`), noise drifting with a clock on a lattice wrapped where a loop's drift ends (`loopDrift`, `vnoiseLoop`), hashed cycle counts wrapped with it (`loopIdx`), the perched birds' flight times moved back at a wrap, the touches' rings aged across one. Frames rendered just past every wrap at once, wrapped and unwrapped, differ by the grain and by sub-pixel edges where the unwrapped clock's own rounding moves things (0.15–0.17 of 255 in 8×8 blocks, the grain's share between any two frames; up to 0.55% of pixels over 6 levels, the rain's streaks), and the frame crossing the wraps changes no more than those either side (10 views, `bench/clocks-look.mjs --wrap`). The sky's clouds (noise in rotated octaves, with no exact period) start again from nothing once their drift is 2000 out (~1–2 days, sooner in a stronger wind), the sky crossfading from the clouds before over 60 s: no jump at the restart, the clouds paler mid-fade. Unwrapped, measured headless by setting the clocks ahead (`setClocks`, 8×8-block change between frames at 60 fps): the river's motion stepped by ~9% from frame to frame after ~8 h (uFlow's float), ~17% after a day, ~40% after 3.5 days, when its ripples also broke into stripes and the grass, stars and lamps moved only every other frame (every fourth after 12 days); the sky's clouds' stills looked unchanged at 116 days. The nobori's ripples ran at time times the gusting wind, so they sped up as the clock grew: 3.5× the frame-to-frame change after 17 minutes; their phase is now the clock's plus the wind's run's. Wrapped, motion stays even at every offset to 12 days. Cost: +0.2% of shader code (+19 KB over all of high's WebGL shaders), the sky's second cloud field only during a restart's fade; not timed.

## Cross-GPU correctness

- `pow` of a slightly negative base gives NaN on Apple/Mali/Adreno, and bloom spreads it. Clamp the base.
- WebGPU has only 1× or 4× MSAA. Compatibility mode has none (so no alpha-to-coverage), a 4096 texture limit, and on Android no shadow comparison sampler (shadows off there).
- Stay within WebGPU default limits (e.g. 8 vertex buffers: the blossom attributes are one interleaved buffer for that reason) and gate optional features.
- Hash value-noise corners from exact `floor()` inputs; the `i + (1,0)` form tore the water on WebGPU.
- Procedural canvas textures use `willReadFrequently` (CPU raster). GPU-rasterized 2D canvases differ per GPU and per context type.
- WebGPU compatibility mode (older GPUs; Android on OpenGL ES) has no storage buffers in vertex shaders and four per compute shader by default: the petals run on the CPU there, as on WebGL. On Android three cannot sample depth with comparison, so its fallback declares a depth texture that keeps a compare function for comparison and samples it without, which compatibility mode rejects: the valley's shadow map (`sunshadow.js`) drops its compare function and filtering there (shadows are off there anyway). Before that, every lit material's pipeline failed and `compileAsync` never resolved: the page hung on its loading screen (checked headed on the RTX 2060 with the compatibility limits forced and an Android user agent: `bench/device-probe.mjs compat`).
- A lost device (`renderer.onDeviceLost`) stops the loop and the page says so with what may still work (Low, the WebGL renderer, a reload); three would otherwise go on drawing nothing.
- Chrome on Windows ignores WebGPU `powerPreference`; three r186's WebGL2 backend never passes it, so the engine creates the WebGL2 context itself with `'high-performance'`. See `docs/research/gpu-selection-and-compat.md`.

## Tried and rejected

- **Temporal AA (`traa`, `taau`):** `traa` blurs swaying foliage and loses alpha-to-coverage; `taau` blurs even at scale 1. At full resolution it costs more than the MSAA it replaces, so it only pays below scale 1, which fails the gate. (Below scale 1 the adaptive quality now upscales with FSR 1, which is spatial: Adaptive quality.)
- **Cached static shadow map:** pixel-identical but a net loss, because restoring the depth copy costs more than drawing the static casters.
- **A running clock** (a minute per second; removed 2026-10-02, owner): the sun moved every frame, so each redraw of the sun's shadow map (every other frame, for the wind) rasterized the casters on a slightly turned texel grid and small ones (flowers, leaves) blinked in and out of shadow; the valley's map and the sky were redone continually too. The time of day is now fixed between time-lapses (CLAUDE.md).
- **Cheaper shadow filter:** 1 tap vs 9 saves only 0.1 ms.
- **MSAA 2× at DPR 2** and **8-bit target after tone mapping:** fail the visual gate (banding, edges).
- **Start-up GPU micro-benchmark for the tier:** GPU clocks ramp over ~1 s of load, so a short benchmark measures clock state rather than GPU class.
- **Volumetric clouds** (2026-10-03, all reverted; the sky keeps its painted clouds). Three tries, all disliked by the owner: (1) a raymarch per sky pixel through a 64³ Perlin-Worley noise: 1.17–1.21× GPU at 1080p on the RTX 2060 and soft, blurry blobs; (2) 16 cumulus baked at start-up into pictures lit from six directions, drawn as camera-facing cards: 0.78–0.84× against (1) but "floating banners" (flat cut-outs, straight bottoms, card edges, even shading); (3) the raymarch into a sky panorama, a thirty-second of its rows per frame with temporal blending: 0.84× against (1), real volume, still disliked. Lessons if clouds are tried again: Worley noise is high at its cells' middles, so erode by one minus it for cauliflower lumps (eroding by it carves smoke); the noise's channels bunch round 0.5, so stretch them before eroding; leave the billows out of the light samples and the clouds look smooth; a weighted sum of six-way light maps lights cloud bases with its front share (multiply transmittances instead); check clouds in full-resolution crops, not downscaled montages. The code is in git history (53515a3, a1a2dd8, db7be81).

- **Render bundles (`BundleGroup`) for the static scenery** (2026-10-02, reverted the same day): 0.79× CPU per frame on WebGPU, but the scenery jumped about while the camera moved (upside-down woods above the horizon, boulders floating). The river's reflection and the sun's shadow map render as nested passes inside the main pass (`updateBefore`), and their copies of each object share the main pass's camera uniform buffers; the bundle's draws run when the main pass ends, so they read the camera that wrote last, often the reflection's mirrored one. Stills don't show it (and headless looks use WebGL, which has no bundles). A bundle is only safe here if nothing renders in the middle of the pass that replays it.

- **GPU-driven culling (compute-built instance lists, indirect draws) for the grass:** not built (2026-10-02). Hiding the grass altogether saved only 0.92–0.95× GPU (~0.4 ms; WebGPU, 1080p, hero/cine250/cine750, three runs, CIs spanning 1), and culling could win back only part of that. Rain and sparks already run entirely in the vertex stage, so compute would gain them nothing.

## Open risks

- The blossom prepass relies on a 4-unit depth margin between two vertex shaders. A compiler that rounds them more differently would punch holes in the canopy.
- `sin`-based hashes with large arguments may band on mobile GPUs.
- The running clocks wrap (Bounded clocks, above): a shader use of `uTime`, `uFlow` or `uWindRun` that does not repeat exactly over its loop jumps at every wrap. `bench/clocks-look.mjs --wrap` shows it.

## Ultra

A tier above high (owner, 2026-10-03) for brief wow moments on strong GPUs: it has no frame-time target, Auto never picks it (`detectTier` does not return it), and chosen by hand it runs at a fixed quality like any hand-picked tier (no adaptive controller). Its limit is GPU memory: the owner's RTX 2060 has 6 GB, and the page stays under ~4 GB at the owner's 1920×1080.

- **What it sets** (`TIERS.ultra` in `src/main.js`): the scene rendered at 1.5× the drawing buffer each way (device pixel ratio up to 3; less where the texture limit requires) and resolved after tone mapping by a Lanczos-2 downsample (`downsample` in `src/post.js`, 36 taps at 1.5×) before the grade; the grade's sharpening follows the scene's pixel density (0.45 at DPR 1 instead of 0.6), since the Lanczos kernel sharpens a little itself. The stars (`uStarPx` in `src/sky.js`), the pollen motes and the lantern halos are sized in the scene pass's pixels, so they are scaled to keep their size on screen. MSAA 4×. The sun's sharp shadow map and the valley's at 8192 (the device's limit if lower); impostor cells 192 px (atlases 4608×3072); flower and leaf atlases 2048²; anisotropy 16 on the flower, bark and leaf textures; near flowers within 9 m; terrain grid 600×630; grass with 2× high's blades (`GRASS_TIERS.ultra`: finer cells, full density to 14 m, narrower blades, more segments) out to 300 m; turf 4,500, wildflowers 4,000, petals 5,400 (13,500 on the GPU), fallen 5,600, motes 800, koi 16, rain 36,000; the reflection at the drawing buffer's resolution; 64 light-shaft samples; `lodScale` 1.7 and `nearModels` for the Blender models (`src/lods.js`).
- **Memory** (headed WebGPU, 1920×1080 canvas, nvidia-smi's total minus the idle baseline): ~1.8 GB steady over the hero, night, near-tree and cine250 views and a time-lapse, 1.9 GB at peak; high 0.8 GB. By size: each 8192 shadow map has a 256 MB depth texture plus a colour target (three's for the sun's map, RGBA8, 256 MB; the valley's is R8, 64 MB), the supersampled scene pass (2880×1620, 4× MSAA, half float, with depth and resolves) ~0.3 GB, the impostor atlases with mips and their depth buffers ~0.26 GB.
- **Frame time** (headed WebGPU, 1920×1080, hurrying down the first lane in walk mode, 360 frames): 9.0 ms GPU median, 13.0 ms p90, 8.5 ms CPU (high: 5.1 / 6.2 / 4.4). No target applies; it is well inside 60 Hz on the RTX 2060.
- **Start-up:** generation 1.2–1.3 → 1.5–1.6 s on 4 workers, the terrain job (the longest) 0.8–0.9 → 1.5–1.6 s; `createMs` 3.8 → 4.1 s headless WebGL at 1280×720, 5.3 → 7.0 s headed WebGPU at 1920×1080.
- **Look** (headless WebGL crops against high): edges are clean where high's stair-step (ropes, posts, twigs, lantern ribs, the pagoda), the grass is finer and denser, the reflection sharper. Texture detail at the scale of a pixel reads a little softer than high's, whose aliasing and stronger sharpening add pixel-level contrast.
- **Rejected:** letting the browser scale a canvas 1.5× larger (a bilinear blit; tried as one bilinear tap in the downsample pass, it aliased: it reads 4 of the ~2.25×2.25 texels under a pixel), an area-weighted box and a tent (both softer than Lanczos on the same crops); impostor cells of 256 px (~0.2 GB more: with the impostors from ~240 m at `lodScale` 1.7 a 10–20 m tree's card is ~60–120 px tall at 1080p, within what 192 px cells hold). GPU time is not measured (no target).

## Bench tools

`bench/` holds the harness from this work: `bench/device-probe.mjs` (loads the built site as a visitor would on an emulated weaker device: WebGPU in compatibility mode, SwiftShader, WebGL, a throttled CPU, a lost context; reports the load, its stages, long tasks, memory and the page's messages), `bench/shaders.mjs` (a tier's shaders by size, per material and object), `pnpm bench` (GPU/CPU timing, `bench/perf.mjs`), `pnpm bench:visual` (NVIDIA ꟻLIP against goldens, `bench/visual.mjs` + `flip_eval.py`), `bench/build.mjs` (builds any commit from a worktree), `bench/passes.mjs` (per-pass GPU times), `bench/hashcheck.mjs` (worker output vs main thread), `bench/stats.mjs` (triangles per object at given views, no images). `bench/passes.mjs --backend webgl` times headless (WebGPU timestamps come back empty there). The goldens (`bench/goldens/`, local to this machine: gitignored) are the scene at b3ab8c3, rendered headless on WebGL by `BENCH_HEADLESS=1 node bench/visual.mjs --build <label> --golden` (over 10 minutes); render them again after a deliberate change to the look. Timing gotchas:

- WebGPU blocks in `writeBuffer`/`getCurrentTexture` when the GPU is behind, so wall-clock "CPU" time is really GPU wait. Time CPU in a GPU-drained batch.
- WebGPU pass timestamps miss work between passes (texture copies). Measure a frame as the span from the first pass's begin to the last pass's end.
- The RTX 2060 here drifts 1680–1905 MHz under thermal limits, so A/B runs need interleaving or a locked clock.
